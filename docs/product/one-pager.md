# How this becomes a product a developer would pay for (draft, polished in Phase 6)

**The problem.** Every tender now has its own rules: peak-hour supply, round-the-clock supply,
batteries, special-zone buyers like this green hydrogen tender. Each comes with its own penalties,
grid deadlines and exceptions. A small bid team handling many tenders a year cannot check every
detail by hand, and one missed detail can lose the bid or cost 25 years of profit.

**Why not just Excel.** They keep Excel; we read it. Excel computes whatever is typed and never says
it is wrong. In a reverse auction the dangerous errors are the ones that make you look cheaper,
because those are the bids that win. Excel also gives return at a price, not the chance of winning
at that price.

**What they pay for.**
- Tender facts that move the price: in this tender, imported panels are allowed; at a mock quote
  that is INR 70 lakh/MW less capex and 37 paise lower breakeven.
- Win odds under the real rules (the 2% band, the 80% rule, partial awards).
- Problems in their own model, priced: here the missing insurance line is 4 paise.
- Discipline in the war room: the board's floor, held, with instant answers.

**The unit.** One paisa on this 200 MW bid is about INR 1.3 lakh per MW of equity value.

**Pricing.** Free model check (upload an old model, see its problems); pilot INR 3 lakh for one
live tender; Growth INR 24 lakh a year (up to 12 tenders, about INR 1,000/MW per bid); Enterprise
INR 60 lakh to 1.2 Cr a year (self-hosted, unlimited, live war room); setup as a service.

**Honest ceiling.** Perhaps 40 to 80 groups bid regularly in India (our estimate), so the bid niche
alone is a good business, not a big one. Growth: hybrid and FDRE tenders, lenders and model
auditors, C&I PPAs, other reverse-auction markets.
